## Story

After fifty years, with the widespread adoption of the Internet, the virtual world and the real world began to run in parallel. However, this development also brought side effects. People who had been immersed in the online world for long periods started exhibiting symptoms of a condition called Reality-Virtual Confusion Irritability Syndrome (RVCIS). Those afflicted with RVCIS became unable to distinguish between virtual and real life. During episodes, they lost the ability to communicate normally with others in reality. Even outside of episodes, they became increasingly radical, leading to frequent cases of violence, self-harm, and suicide. Dissatisfied with the restrictions of the real world compared to the limitless possibilities of the virtual world, many of these individuals began openly opposing the government and legal system. It took only a few years for this condition to spread from isolated cases to a large-scale outbreak, throwing society into turmoil.

During this period, a New Liberty Party emerged. The party’s leader was the first documented case of RVCIS. A publicly exposed the government’s persecution of him and declared himself a “New Human,” arguing that RVCIS was not a disorder but rather an essential step in human evolution—from the physical world into the virtual world. He claimed that New Humans possessed superior healing abilities and greater tolerance for virtual reality. His team even developed a panel-based communication system designed specifically for New Humans.

The New Liberty Party quickly gained widespread support. Eventually, the old government collapsed, and a new government was established under the party’s rule. To facilitate human “evolution,” the New Liberty Party decided to rebuild the world to accommodate the needs of New Humans. As a result, the New World gradually expanded outward from the New Liberty Party’s center. To accelerate the transformation, they developed machines that destroyed remnants of the Old World and rebuilt them. One of the machine they invented called “NEW AGE”, which was deployed at newly reconstructed sites.

At each reconstruction point, former citizens of the Old World were subjected to a “training” process. Only those who successfully evolved into New Humans were allowed to enter and live in the New World. Those who failed or refused to comply were exiled.

However, among the exiled and those who opposed the New Liberty Party, a resistance organization called the “Guardians” (THE GUARD) was formed. Their mission was to sabotage the New World and resist its expansion, striving to reclaim the remnants of the Old World.

## Design notes

### When the virtual world feels more real than reality

Fifty years into a networked future, people begin to experience Reality–Virtual Confusion Irritability Syndrome. What society calls a disorder is reframed by a political movement as the next step in human evolution.

The project uses fiction to explore a familiar tension: who gets to define normality when technology changes the boundaries of identity and experience?

### Storytelling as a design method

Panels, symbols, propaganda, and character perspectives form a speculative communication system for the imagined “New Humans.”

Rather than predicting one future, the work creates a world through which present-day anxieties about immersion, governance, and belonging can be examined.

### A world organized around virtual life

The portfolio locates the story in 2090, after ecological decline and increased ultraviolet exposure have made everyday physical life more difficult. The New Age promises a different future through virtual experience. Identity and assets are stored on a chip, linking personal life to the infrastructure that sustains it.

C37, a spy from The Guard, enters this system. The narrative follows an investigation through living cells, a factory, and a laboratory, using the character’s choices to expose the gap between the promise of liberation and the mechanisms of control.

### Designing the infrastructure behind the fiction

The living cell is developed as an architectural mechanism, with pipelines, a magnetic plate that simulates bodily actions, and movable shells on tracks. Drawings connect this intimate apparatus to larger systems: a residential district, water and farming cells, robot infrastructure, and an AZ317 helicopter.

These details give the speculative world spatial rules. The reader can see how belief is supported by housing, logistics, and the organization of bodies, rather than encountering the future only as a visual atmosphere.
